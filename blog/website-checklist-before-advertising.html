12 things to check on your website before you advertise

Ads send people to your website. If the site is slow, hard to use or can't measure what happens next, you pay for every visit and learn nothing. Check these twelve things first.

Measure

1. Analytics is installed and working

Install Google Analytics 4 (or a privacy-friendly alternative) on every page. Then open its real-time report and visit your own site to confirm the visit shows up.

2. Conversions are tracked

Decide what counts as a result: a form sent, a call, a purchase. Track it as a key event, and connect it to your ad platform so you can see which ads bring customers, not just clicks.

3. Consent is handled

If you have visitors from the EU or UK, analytics and ad cookies need their consent. Use a cookie banner that actually blocks tracking until people agree.

Get found

4. Search Console is set up

Verify your domain in Google Search Console and submit a sitemap. It tells you which pages Google has indexed and which searches show your site.

5. One address for your site

Pick one version of your domain, with or without www, and redirect the other to it. Otherwise visitors who type the other version can hit an error, and search engines may split your pages between two addresses.

6. Every page has a clear title and description

These are what people see in search results. Write them for humans: what the page offers, in under 60 characters for the title.

7. Links look good when shared

Add Open Graph tags so a link posted on LinkedIn or in a chat shows your title, summary and image instead of a bare URL.

Convert

8. It is fast on a phone

Most ad clicks come from phones. Check Google's Core Web Vitals in PageSpeed Insights: the main content should appear within 2.5 seconds, taps should respond within 200 milliseconds, and the layout shouldn't jump around while it loads.

9. The next step is obvious

Every landing page needs one clear action, visible without scrolling: book a call, get a quote, buy. Make it easy to contact you from anywhere on the site.

10. Broken links lead somewhere useful

A mistyped address should show a helpful "page not found" page with a way back, not a blank screen.

Protect

11. HTTPS everywhere

Every page should load over HTTPS, with plain HTTP redirecting to it. Browsers warn visitors about forms on insecure pages.

12. Your email is trusted

Leads will reply to your emails. Make sure your domain has SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up so your replies don't land in spam.

Then launch, and watch

Start with a small budget, check analytics daily for the first week, and fix what you see before you scale up. The checklist is quick. Skipping it is expensive.
